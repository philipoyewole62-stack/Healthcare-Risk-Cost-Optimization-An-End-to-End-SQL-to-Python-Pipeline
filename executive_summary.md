# Executive Summary

## Objective
The objective of this analysis is to identify **high-risk hospitalizations** that disproportionately contribute to healthcare cost exposure, using Length of Stay (LOS) as a practical proxy for financial risk.

---

## Key Insights

- Hospital cost risk is **highly concentrated**, not evenly distributed.
- Length of Stay is tightly linked to total cost exposure, reinforcing its usefulness as a risk proxy.
- Emergency admissions exhibit higher volatility and greater tail risk compared to other admission types.
- Individual clinical abnormality flags alone do not fully explain cost exposure, but **compounded risk signals** increase hospitalization duration.
- A small subset of admissions accounts for a disproportionate share of estimated costs.

---

## Why Averages Fail

Average length of stay masks extreme outcomes that drive operational strain and financial overruns.  
This analysis focuses on **percentile-based thresholds** and volatility to surface hidden risk that traditional summary statistics overlook.

---

## Business Implications

- Early identification of high-risk admissions enables proactive resource allocation.
- Risk stratification can support cost containment strategies without compromising patient care.
- Clinical signals, when combined with admission context, offer actionable insights for intervention.

---

## Next Steps

With access to additional data, this framework could be extended to:
- Predictive risk scoring at admission
- Time-series monitoring of patient deterioration
- Integration with hospital capacity planning systems
